## Slide 5
Valuation Methods
Most (but not all) M&V plans describe how to “value” estimated reductions in dependent variables.
Valuation methods can range from incredibly simple—for example, $0.12 per kWh saved—to incredibly complex.
Stakeholders should agree upon the valuation method in advance of implementation.
Whatever the valuation method, it should be applied to both the adjusted baseline and measured reporting period values.

## Slide 9
Why Load Shape Matters Now
In an era of time-varying rates and real-time carbon accounting
Energy rates vary by time-of-use (TOU), season, and demand charges
A kWh saved during peak hours is more valuable than off-peak
Load shape reveals the distribution of energy use over time
Traditional M&V (total kWh) is insufficient for modern rate structures
WHEN you save matters as much as HOW MUCH you save
Example: California moved to mandatory TOU rates. Same kWh savings now worth 2-3x more if timed correctly.

## Slide 14
CO2 Emissions Calculations
Carbon dioxide emissions equivalents of the energy savings from a project can be calculated using standard conversion factors.
The conversion factor will vary depending on the resource type—electricity, gas, or renewable energy—and may vary based on time of day and other factors as well.
More real time carbon attribution is now available.

## Slide 21
Utility Program Considerations
Utility program reporting can occur at the end of a program cycle, a program year, or project implementation. This process typically consists of two main components:
A descriptive report outlining relevant customer information, project information, and M&V methodology
A calculation file containing all relevant raw data, data transformations or corrections, calculations, and explanations

## Slide 22
Key Takeaways
The report is the primary deliverable of most M&V activities.
An M&V report has two basic objectives: to justify reported savings values and address any non-routine adjustments.
CMVPs must increasingly tailor their reports to multiple audiences, including ESG and Carbon emissions reporting

## Slide 24
Conclusion
M&V is either/both performance verification and savings determination.
The goal of M&V is a successful settlement.
Savings determination relies on the counterfactual method.
The counterfactual method relies on a model of the baseline.
Context matters. Who will review the results? What do they require?
There are many judgments a CMVP needs to make.
There is always uncertainty. Knowing how much can be useful.
The role of the CMVP is to build and guide a team over the many hurdles to arrive at successful settlement.
